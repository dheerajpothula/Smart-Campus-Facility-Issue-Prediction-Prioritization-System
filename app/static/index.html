Frequently Asked Questions

Guidance on campus facilities and repair workflows

How long does it take for a complaint to be attended?

Critical issues (such as water overflow or power outages) are typically inspected within 2 to 4 hours. General facility defects (such as classroom furniture or flickering lights) are handled within 24 to 48 hours according to campus SLA.

How does the system decide complaint priority?

Our AI platform calculates a weighted priority score based on severity, repeat occurrences at that exact location, student footfall density, and location criticality.

Can other students see my tickets?

No. The student portal enforces strict privacy isolation. You only see issues filed by your student account.

Can I reopen an issue if it was not repaired properly?

Yes, if an issue recurs, simply report it again. The system detects repeated occurrences and will automatically escalate the ticket to high recurrence priority!

How the Intelligent Maintenance System Operates

5 automated stages converting raw student feedback into optimized physical maintenance dispatches

1

Step 1 — Submit Complaint

User Interaction

A student, professor, or staff member encounters a facility defect (e.g., leaking water cooler, flickering light, jammed lift). They submit a natural-language description along with the location (e.g. "Block C 2nd floor").

Input Example: "The water cooler near Block C 2nd floor has stopped working again and water is leaking on the floor during lunch hours."
2

Step 2 — Understand (NLP & Entity Extraction)

Scikit-Learn NLP Pipeline

The Natural Language Processing engine cleans and vectorizes the text using unigram/bigram TF-IDF representations. A multi-class classifier identifies the facility domain (Water Cooler, Washroom, Lighting, HVAC, etc.) with calibrated probabilities.

Category: Water Cooler (96% Confidence) Keywords: ["water", "cooler", "leaking", "lunch", "floor"]
3

Step 3 — Predict Severity, Recurrence & Location Risk

Multi-Model Inference

Three distinct algorithms process the contextual features:

  • Severity Model: Detects physical safety signals, water damage, or electrical hazards → Severity: High (85 pts)
  • Recurrence Engine: Scans the campus repository for matching facility/location pairs → 12 Prior Complaints Detected (Chronic)
  • Location Risk Model: Maps pedestrian traffic telemetry (Block C has 88/100 footfall density during lunch) → Location Risk: High (84 pts)
4

Step 4 — Calculate Explainable Priority Score (0 - 100)

Configurable Scoring Engine

Rather than an arbitrary black-box number, the priority score combines weighted factors:

$$\text{Priority} = 0.35(\text{Severity}) + 0.25(\text{Recurrence}) + 0.20(\text{Footfall}) + 0.10(\text{Location Risk}) + 0.10(\text{Impact}) = \mathbf{85.6 / 100}$$
5

Step 5 — Action & Work Order Dispatch

Operational Resolution

The maintenance team receives an automated actionable work order in their priority dispatch queue with the exact diagnosis: "Emergency dispatch: overhaul compressor & repair drainage line; schedule full filtration unit replacement."
